A crop measured in years, not seasons

Sandalwood cannot be hurried. It will not even feed itself — the roots have to reach a neighbour's. So the grove gets its own page on this site, and an update every year on the channel.

6 yearsSince the first saplings went in

Planted in July 2019. Heartwood — the fragrant part, the only part worth anything — is still about nine years away. Everything below is where the grove is now, and what comes next.

  1. Year 1

    Saplings, with a host beside each one

    Sandalwood is a part-parasite. Every sapling went in next to red gram, so the young roots had something to attach to within the first year. Pits dug wide, spacing kept generous.

  2. Year 2–3

    Swapping to a host that lasts

    Red gram finishes its life quickly, so a longer-lived host goes in alongside — curry leaf and casuarina here. Watering stays light. Grazing animals are the real danger at this height.

  3. Year 5

    Girth, and the first count of losses

    Not every tree makes it. This is the year I measured each trunk, wrote the numbers in a notebook, and put that notebook on camera — the failures included.

  4. Year 8–10

    Heartwood begins, out of sight

    The colour and the scent start forming in the core while the outside looks the same. Nothing to do but protect the trees and keep the record.

  5. Year 15

    The wait ends

    Harvest, if the trees have earned it. In India, felling and moving sandalwood needs clearance from the forest department even when the trees are your own — so that paperwork gets filmed too.

Anyone planting sandalwood should check the current rules with their own state forest department before the first sapling goes in. What is shown here is one farm's experience, not advice.

About

Why I started filming

I have farmed this land for most of my life and cooked from it for longer. When my children moved away they began calling to ask how a dish was made — so I set a phone against a vessel and recorded it once, properly.

That video is still up. Since then the channel has become a record of the whole cycle: what we plant, what survives, what it costs, and what it tastes like at the end. The sandalwood is the slowest thread in it. I will not see the harvest for years, and I would still like the whole wait on record.

“Plant for a year you cannot see, cook what is ready today.” కొంచెం పండించు, బాగా వండు, ఇంకొకరికి నేర్పు.
